# 7-day content plan — from zero to a repeatable channel

**Goal:** publish three useful articles, put them in front of the right
audiences, and turn the attention into GitHub stars, npm installs and sites
joining the adoption index.

This is a distribution plan, not a content-volume plan. Three good articles,
cross-posted properly and followed by real conversations, beat thirty posts
nobody reads.

## Realistic first-week targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---:|
| Articles published | 3 |
| Platforms per article | 2 |
| Total article views | 1,000+ |
| Clicks to GitHub / docs | 100+ |
| New GitHub stars | 25+ |
| npm downloads in the week | 100+ |
| Adopter index submissions | 3+ |
| Newsletter replies | 2+ |

These are first-week targets, not guarantees. Track them daily and adjust.

## Before day 1: the 30-minute setup

- [ ] Create a Medium account (or use your existing one).
- [ ] Add a byline: your name, one line about grapheway.
- [ ] Create a cover image. Use the project OG image or a real screenshot.
- [ ] Create a tracking sheet with these columns:
      date, platform, article, URL, views, reads, CTR, GitHub clicks,
      stars, npm downloads, adopters, notes.
- [ ] Read the three articles once and add one personal sentence to each
      intro. Personal experience beats generic advice.
- [ ] Decide the canonical strategy:
      - If you have a blog on your own domain, publish there first and set
        Medium's canonical URL to that page.
      - If you do not, publish on Medium first and use the Medium URL as the
        canonical for dev.to and Hashnode.
- [ ] Add a short author bio to your Medium, dev.to and Hashnode profiles.
- [ ] Check every link in the article once. Broken links kill trust.

## The 7-day calendar

### Day 1 — Publish Article 1 (site owners and marketers)

**Article:** `AI Agents Can't Read Your Website: The 2026 Checklist`

- Publish on Medium at 9–10am in your main timezone.
- Use the exact title and meta description from
  [`docs/articles/README.md`](../articles/README.md).
- Tags: Artificial Intelligence, AI Agents, Web Development, SEO, Product.
- Add the cover image and alt text.
- Add the CTA at the end and one link in the first third of the article.
- Share on LinkedIn with a short personal note: “We audited 30 docs sites.
  The average agent-readiness score was 28/100.”
- Share on X with a 3-post thread: the data, the checklist, the audit command.
- Spend 30 minutes replying to every comment.

**Success signal:** 100+ views and 10+ clicks to GitHub or the audit page.

### Day 2 — Publish Article 2 (developers and AI builders)

**Article:** `Turn Any Website Into an MCP Server in One Command`

- Publish on dev.to first (technical audiences live there), then cross-post to
  Medium with the canonical URL set to the dev.to post.
- Tags on dev.to: `mcp`, `ai`, `webdev`, `typescript`.
- Include the hosted MCP URL and the local `npx` command.
- Post to `r/mcp` and `r/ClaudeAI`. Follow each community's self-promotion
  rules; write a discussion-first post, not a drive-by link.
- Reply to comments with the exact command, not a sales pitch.
- Add the article link to the GitHub README's “Learn more” section.

**Success signal:** 250+ views on dev.to and 5+ comments or questions.

### Day 3 — Publish Article 3 (SEO and GEO teams)

**Article:** `llms.txt Is Not Enough: AI Search Needs a Live Graph`

- Publish on Medium in the morning.
- Tags: SEO, Generative AI, Content Strategy, AI Agents, Marketing.
- Share on LinkedIn with the comparison table as a carousel or image.
- Post in one SEO community (not five). Pick the one where you are already a
  member. Ask a question; do not drop a link and leave.
- Send the article to two SEO newsletters as a pitch, not a press release.

**Success signal:** 150+ views and 5+ meaningful replies.

### Day 4 — Cross-post and consolidate

- Cross-post all three articles to Hashnode with canonical URLs.
- Add a “Writing” section to the GitHub README linking the three articles.
- Add the links to your personal site, LinkedIn featured section and X bio.
- Create one short post per article for X and LinkedIn, each with a different
  hook. Do not paste the same text everywhere.
- Update the adoption index with any new sites you personally onboard.

**Success signal:** every article has at least two live URLs and one internal
link back to the docs or readiness index.

### Day 5 — Outreach day

Send **20 personalized messages**, not 200 generic ones.

Targets:

- docs teams at companies with low readiness scores;
- developer-tool founders;
- agencies that build documentation sites;
- newsletter editors.

Use this structure:

> Hi <name> — I ran an agent-readiness audit on <site>: <score>/100. The common
> gaps are a missing discovery card and no MCP endpoint. I built an open-source
> tool that fixes it in one command. I wrote the checklist here: <link>. Happy
> to send the full report or do a 20-minute migration with you — no charge,
> I'm looking for three design partners.

Rules:

- One message per person.
- Reference something real about their site.
- No follow-up more than once.
- Stop if they say no.

**Success signal:** 5 replies and 2 calls booked.

### Day 6 — Repurpose the best-performing piece

Look at the numbers from days 1–3. Pick the article with the highest read
ratio, not the highest views.

- Turn it into a 6-post X thread.
- Turn it into a LinkedIn carousel or a 60-second screen recording.
- Record a short demo: paste a URL, show the graph, connect an MCP client.
- Post the demo with a plain-language caption.
- Submit the technical article to Hacker News **only if** it is genuinely
  useful and not a launch ad. Use a descriptive title, not clickbait.

**Success signal:** one repurposed asset outperforms the original post.

### Day 7 — Measure, fix, repeat

- Fill in the tracking sheet.
- Answer every unanswered comment.
- Update the articles with the questions people asked (this improves SEO and
  saves the next reader time).
- Add one internal link from each article to the most relevant other article.
- Double down on the platform that sent the most qualified traffic.
- Write down the one thing you will change next week.

**Success signal:** a clear answer to “which channel sent people who actually
installed or starred?”

## SEO checklist for each article

- [ ] Primary keyword in the title, H1, first 100 words and one H2.
- [ ] Meta description under 155 characters.
- [ ] One H1; descriptive H2s; short paragraphs.
- [ ] 1,000–1,500 words of real substance.
- [ ] At least 3 internal links and 2 authoritative external links.
- [ ] A table, checklist or numbered list for scannability.
- [ ] FAQ section with the questions people actually ask.
- [ ] Cover image with descriptive alt text.
- [ ] Canonical URL set when cross-posting.
- [ ] No keyword stuffing, no fake statistics, no exaggerated claims.
- [ ] Author bio and affiliation disclosed.

## Metrics that matter

Ignore vanity metrics for decisions. Watch these:

1. **Read ratio** — did people finish the article?
2. **Click-through to GitHub or docs** — did they take the next step?
3. **Stars and installs** — did they try it?
4. **Adopter submissions** — did they adopt it?
5. **Replies and calls** — did it start a relationship?

A post with 2,000 views and 5 clicks is worse than a post with 300 views and
40 clicks.

## What not to do

- Do not buy stars, downloads, views or comments.
- Do not post the same link in ten communities in one hour.
- Do not use a fake account or a fake testimonial.
- Do not promise “#1 in AI search” or a specific ranking.
- Do not hide that you built the tool.
- Do not cross-post without a canonical URL.

## After day 7

If one article works, turn it into a series:

- “Agent readiness for docs teams”
- “MCP for e-commerce”
- “How to audit 100 sites in an afternoon”
- “What agents do after they find your page”

The goal is not three articles. It is one repeatable channel that brings the
right people to the repo every week.
